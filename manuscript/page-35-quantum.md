## Quantum technologies: the realistic roles

Between "quantum computing will change everything" and "quantum is a fad" lies a third position — the *already working* one ::est:: [R13].

### What works today ::est::

- **Atomic clocks.** Optical clocks now keep time to ~1 part in 10¹⁸ — the physics behind GPS, network timing and tests of fundamental constants [R13].
- **Quantum sensors.** Atom interferometers and magnetometers are used in laboratories and field trials for gravity and magnetic measurements ◆.
- **Quantum key distribution.** Point-to-point secure links demonstrated over fibre and satellite ◆.

### What's in research ◆

- **Quantum computers** that outperform classical machines on *useful* problems — partially demonstrated on contrived benchmarks; general-purpose utility remains open.
- **Quantum networks** beyond metro-scale links.

### The 2050 honest forecast ▲

- Clocks, sensors and timing: embedded everywhere today ::est:: — and still growing ▲
- Quantum computing: powerful for specialised domains, not a general revolution — if history rhymes with classical computing's first decades ▲

**Going deeper.** Note what quantum tech does *for astronomy*: better clocks sharpen very-long-baseline interferometry ::est:: [R13]; better sensors may one day fly on gravity-mapping missions ▲. The tools improve our *instruments* — our theme again.

**Advanced corner.** Why clocks matter to physics: comparing clocks over a year can detect a drift of fundamental constants at ~10⁻¹⁸ level — so far: no drift found ::est::, a quiet but profound null result.

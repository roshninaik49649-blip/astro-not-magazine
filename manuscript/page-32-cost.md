## The cost of exploration

**Intro.** Space budgets are public money with public justification. What does exploration actually cost, and what does it buy?

**Scale of the spending ::est:: [R21].** NASA's annual budget is on the order of tens of billions of US dollars — roughly one half-penny of the US federal budget per dollar. ISRO operates on a budget that is smaller by an order of magnitude, yet delivers planetary missions [R09]. Budget *size* doesn't map cleanly to mission *ambition*.

**Where the money goes ▲:**
- **People** — the majority of any space programme's cost is salaries of engineers and scientists ::est::
- **Hardware & launch** — dominated by launch mass; reusability is reshaping this ◆
- **Operations** — ground networks, data pipelines, decades-long archives

**The sustainability ledger ◆ [R14]:** every launch adds debris risk to shared orbits; every mega-constellation adds collision-avoidance load. Cleanup, deorbit rules and traffic coordination are now budget lines ::est:: — exploration's bill includes housekeeping.

**Public value — the honest case ▲:** satellites for weather, navigation and communications return value daily ::est::; fundamental science returns understanding at uncertain, occasionally enormous, long-term rates.

**Advanced corner.** Compare *cost per scientific result* across programmes: sample-return, survey telescopes and small planetary missions score very differently.

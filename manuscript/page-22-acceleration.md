## The accelerating universe

**Intro.** In 1998, two teams measuring distant supernovae found the Universe isn't just expanding — the expansion is speeding up ::est:: [R08]. Something, labelled **dark energy**, pushes space apart. It accounts for ~68 % of the Universe's energy budget ::est:: [R05].

**Going deeper.** "Dark energy" is a label for behaviour, not an explanation. The standard model (ΛCDM) treats it as a constant — the cosmological constant ::est:: — which fits all current data well [R05]. The deepest open question ◆: is it truly constant, evolving, or an illusion of incomplete theory? New surveys (Euclid, Rubin) are built to test exactly this [R11] ◆.

**Modelling uncertainty — the honest part.** Competing models (constant, evolving "w", interacting) fit within current error bars ◆. Their forecasts for 2050 diverge mainly in *precision*, not in whether expansion accelerates — on that, every viable model agrees ::est::.

**Advanced corner.** The SIMULATED diagram plots H(z), the expansion rate versus redshift; the orange ΛCDM curve is the fitted model [R05]. Distinguishing a constant from an evolving component requires percent-level precision over a huge redshift range — a decade-long job ▲.

**Caption.** 2050 forecast ▲: expansion continues accelerating ::est::; the *why* may still be open.

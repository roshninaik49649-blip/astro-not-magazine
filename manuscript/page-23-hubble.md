## The Hubble tension

**Intro.** How fast is the Universe expanding? Ask by measuring nearby distances and motions (the distance ladder, SH0ES): H₀ ≈ 73 km/s/Mpc [R06]. Ask by modelling the infant Universe (Planck's cosmic microwave background): H₀ ≈ 67.4 [R05]. The disagreement is ~5σ ::est:: — far beyond expected error bars. This is the sharpest unexplained tension in modern cosmology ◆.

**Going deeper.** Both measurements are exquisitely careful ::est::. That's why the tension is interesting: a real disagreement hints that one of our assumptions — in the ladder's calibration, in the early-Universe model, or in physics between them — is incomplete ◆.

**Candidate resolutions on the table ◆:**

- A hidden early-universe ingredient (e.g. brief extra radiation epoch)
- Systematics buried in distance-ladder steps (Cepheids, TRGB, SNe)
- New neutrino physics or dark-sector interactions

**Advanced corner.** The figure compares three anchor methods with error bars: note the non-overlapping ranges. "Independent ladder with TRGB anchors" sits between them [R06] — part of why the case isn't closed.

**2050 forecast ▲:** the tension is either resolved (error found or physics found) or sharpened into the decade's defining puzzle. Either outcome is *our understanding* changing — not the Universe ::est::.

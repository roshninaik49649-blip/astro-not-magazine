## What if we find something?

**Intro.** Suppose a spectrum shows the combination nobody can yet explain without life. What happens next is a playbook ::est:: — because science has rehearsed caution in other fields.

:::ladder The verification ladder — climb before you announce
<span class="n">1</span> **Re-observe** — different teams, different telescopes, different seasons ::est:: (p 24).

<span class="n">2</span> **Kill the alternatives** — clouds, chemistry, instrumental artefacts get their day in court ◆.

<span class="n">3</span> **Independent analysis** — raw data, rival methods, adversarial review ◆.

<span class="n">4</span> **Cautious language** — "candidate", "consistent with", "not yet explained" ::est::.
:::

:::callout What a responsible 2050 announcement sounds like ▲
A responsible announcement names the uncertainty and the prior expectations; a viral one names only the headline. The magazine's advice: judge a claim by its error bars, not its italics.
:::

**Going deeper.** History's rehearsed near-misses: the 1996 "martian meteorite" organics debate and decades of pulsar-signal false alarms taught the field to separate *interesting* from *confirmed* ◆ [R17, R20].

**Advanced corner.** Protocol design is now a research topic itself: some teams pre-register biosignature analysis plans before data arrives, so they cannot be tuned to a result ◆.

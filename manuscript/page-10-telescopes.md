## The telescope revolution

**Intro.** Every generation of astronomers believes it lives in a golden age of telescopes — and every generation has been right. Sensitivity (how faint an object you can see) and resolution (how fine a detail you can separate) have improved by orders of magnitude per generation ::est:: [R02].

**Going deeper.** Three levers buy performance:

1. **Collecting area.** Light gathered scales with mirror area; the jump from 2.4 m (Hubble) to 6.5 m (JWST) was a seven-fold leap in collecting power ::est:: [R02]. Thirty-metre ground telescopes are in construction ◆.
2. **Wavelength access.** New windows open new Universes: radio in the 1930s–50s, X-ray and infrared from space in the 1960s–2000s (p 11).
3. **Interference tricks.** Interferometers combine many telescopes into one virtual instrument with kilometre-scale resolution ::est:: — the trick behind both radio VLBI and the Event Horizon Telescope (p 18).

**Advanced corner.** Diffraction sets a hard floor: angular resolution ≈ 1.22 λ/D. It's why infrared telescopes in space beat bigger optical scopes on the ground, and why survey cadence — how often the sky is revisited — is now as precious as sensitivity [R12]. By 2050 ▲, expect wider fields, faster cadence and petabyte-scale pipelines (p 33) rather than one magical "super-scope".

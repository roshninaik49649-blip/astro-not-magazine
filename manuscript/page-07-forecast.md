## What might we discover?

A qualified forecast for 2026–2050, labelled honestly. None of this is guaranteed; all of it is the kind of result working astronomers would bet on.

### Reasonably likely ▲

- **A habitable-zone planet with a scrutinised atmosphere.** Telescopes now flying will push transit spectroscopy to smaller worlds [R01, R02]. Expect candidates — and fierce debate about what their spectra mean (pp 12–14).
- **Thousands of new gravitational-wave detections ▲** as detector networks widen [R17], plus rarer signals: neutron-star–black-hole pairs, possibly continuous waves from spinning pulsars.
- **A dark-matter/energy data flood ▲.** Euclid and the Rubin Observatory's decade-long survey will map billions of galaxies [R11, R12]. Whether this *solves* dark energy is genuinely unknown ◆.

### Possible but not promised ▲

- **A biosignature candidate.** Something ambiguous, hotly contested, probably abiotic false positives included (p 14).
- **A crack in ΛCDM.** The Hubble tension (p 23) is already a crack-shaped mystery ◆; new surveys will either widen or dissolve it.

### Extremely unlikely by 2050 ::est::

- A confirmed alien civilisation, faster-than-light travel, or a change in the laws of physics. Each would need evidence far beyond anything current instruments hint at.

**Going deeper.** Notice the pattern: likely discoveries are *increments* — more planets, more events, tighter error bars — not revolutions. Revolutions (p 39) tend to arrive unannounced.

## No cosmic deadline

Two lists, kept strictly apart. Nothing on the left is a promise; nothing on the right requires new physics.

:::cards
:::card What will NOT change by 2050
- The laws of physics and the constants that shape them ::est:: — no evidence for drift on any testable timescale [R05]
- The Sun's nature: a stable main-sequence star, mid-life ::est::
- The Milky Way's shape and position of the Sun ::est:: — galaxy-scale processes take hundreds of millions of years
- The expansion of the cosmos ::est:: — ongoing for ~5 billion years, unchanged in character [R05, R08]
- The inventory of matter and energy we already know about ::est:: — stars, planets, gas, dust keep being what they are
:::
:::card What plausibly WILL change by 2050
- Our census of planets ▲ — thousands more confirmed exoplanets are a reasonable projection [R01]
- The sensitivity of our instruments ▲ — bigger surveys, space telescopes, detectors
- The catalogue of gravitational-wave events ▲ — from tens towards tens of thousands [R17]
- Human activity in space ▲ — more lunar missions, more stations, more debris to manage [R10, R14]
- Our models' precision ▲ — smaller error bars, sharper tensions, better simulations
:::
:::

:::callout The asymmetry — the point of the whole magazine
Discovery power grows exponentially, but the objects of study evolve on geological or longer timescales. Expect a **data** revolution, not a **cosmos** revolution ::est::.
:::

:::pull
By 2050 the map will be redrawn a hundred times. The territory will not have moved.
:::

## When spacetime rings

**Intro.** Einstein's general relativity predicts that accelerating masses ripple spacetime itself ::est:: [R03]. In September 2015, twin LIGO detectors recorded such a ripple: two black holes, ~30 solar masses each, spiralling together. The signal lasted 0.2 seconds and stretched the detectors by about a thousandth of a proton's width ::est::.

**Going deeper.** The waveform *is* the physics. As the two bodies orbit faster and closer, frequency and amplitude climb — a "chirp". From the chirp, analysts reconstruct the masses, the distance (~1.3 billion light-years for the first event) and even test whether the ringdown matches the black holes general relativity predicts ::est:: [R03].

**Interferometry in brief.** Each LIGO arm is 4 km long; laser light compares the two arms to a precision far finer than an atom ::est:: [R17].

**Advanced corner.** Multi-messenger astronomy: in 2017, a gravitational-wave event was followed within seconds by gamma rays from the same sky patch, then by optical telescopes finding the kilonova ::est:: [R17]. Speed of light and speed of gravity agree to ~1 part in 10¹⁵ — a stunning confirmation of relativity.

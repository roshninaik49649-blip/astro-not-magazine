## The big debate: what would change humanity most by 2050?

Four positions, argued fairly, all tagged ▲ — then it's your turn.

:::cards
:::card A · Seeing life's second origin
A confirmed biosignature candidate [R01] would re-run the deepest question humans have ever asked. Even a contested candidate ◆ could reorient philosophy, religion and research priorities ▲.
:::
:::card B · Becoming multi-planetary
First crewed Mars attempt or permanent lunar presence [R10] ▲ — less about the site, more about proof that humanity's *reach* is no longer single-point-of-failure.
:::
:::card C · Managing our own environment
Orbital debris rules [R14], climate-observing satellites, planetary-defence demonstrations — verified steps ::est:: with plausible growth ▲ — unglamorous governance that makes every other future possible.
:::
:::card D · Machines that think with us
AI-assisted science reaching validation-grade trust ◆ [R12] — changing who (and what) gets to make a discovery.
:::
:::

:::callout The counterweight all four share
None of these changes the Universe. Each changes *humanity's relationship to it* — the magazine's thesis in debate form ::est::.
:::

:::pull
Your move: rank A–D, then defend your **last place** hardest — that's where your assumptions live.
:::

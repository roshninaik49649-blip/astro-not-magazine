## Mars: dream vs reality

**Intro.** Mars is the most habitable *other* planet we know — and it is spectacularly uninhabitable ::est::. The dream sells itself; this page audits the four hard parts.

:::cards
:::card 1 · Radiation ::est:: [R15]
Curiosity's RAD instrument measured surface doses equivalent to a career limit for astronauts within a few years of exposure. Shielding costs mass; mass costs rockets.
:::
:::card 2 · Travel time ▲
Six to nine months each way with current propulsion, plus a long stay — departure windows every ~26 months ::est::.
:::
:::card 3 · Life support at scale
Recycling air and water for a small crew is proven on the ISS ::est:: [R16]; doing it for months or years without resupply, with failure margins, remains unsolved engineering ◆.
:::
:::card 4 · Human health ◆
Partial gravity (0.38 g), dust toxicity, isolation — studied, but not solved.
:::
:::

**Going deeper.** Robotic Mars is thriving ::est:: [R16]: rovers do geology, helicopters fly, sample tubes are cached for return. Everything robotic Mars achieves argues for patience, not against ambition.

**Advanced corner.** Sample return would let Earth's labs test for biosignatures with instruments no rover can carry ◆ — the highest-value Mars science currently in planning ▲.

**Caption.** 2050 verdict ▲: a crewed Mars *landing attempt* is plausible; a self-sustaining city is fiction ✦ — and this magazine keeps that distinction.

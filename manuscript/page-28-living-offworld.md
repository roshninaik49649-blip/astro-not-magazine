## Living away from Earth

What a permanent off-world outpost actually needs — four budgets that never close easily.

:::cards
:::card Power
- Moon night = 14 Earth-days dark ::est:: → nuclear or battery-scale storage ◆
- Solar works near lunar poles (sunlit rims) ::est:: [R10]
:::
:::card Water
- Recycling loops recover ~90 %+ on ISS ::est::
- Local ice (Moon/Mars) is the game-changer — if extraction works economically ◆
:::
:::card Shielding
- Regolith berms (cheap mass) vs water walls (dual use) vs underground ◆
- Cosmic-ray dose limits drive habitat design more than temperature does ::est::
:::
:::card Repair & autonomy
- No 40-minute-rescue call to Earth on Mars; crews must fix what Earth's supply chain fixes here ◆
- The ISS's greatest export may be its maintenance culture ::est::
:::
:::

:::callout The binding constraint: logistics mass, not physics
Every kg sent costs launch energy; every closed loop saves mass forever. "Sustainability" here is literal engineering, not a slogan ::est::.
:::

**Space sustainability, two ways.** For crews: circular resource systems ◆. For orbit itself: debris management — hundreds of thousands of tracked objects now threaten the environment every nation shares ::est:: [R14]. A 2050-era presence in space ▲ will be judged by whether both kinds of housekeeping work.

# CHAPTER 05
## AI searches the stars

Modern astronomy drowns politely. The Rubin Observatory's survey will find ~20 terabytes *per night* [R12] ::est:: — more than a human could inspect in a lifetime, every night, for ten years. Nothing in that flood gets *seen* without software.

So the frontier of discovery now runs through machine learning: classifying millions of objects (p 34), proposing patterns no human would try — and, crucially, being distrusted in exactly the right ways (p 36).

This chapter keeps two threads tight. **Thread one:** AI as an instrument — like a mirror, only statistical ::est:: [R12]. **Thread two:** the human role is not shrinking; it is moving to the parts machines can't do — validation, judgement, and noticing when the pattern is an artefact ::est:: (pp 34, 36).

**Key idea:** the telescope of 2050 is a *pipeline* ▲ — and pipelines have strengths, biases and failure modes we must learn to audit.

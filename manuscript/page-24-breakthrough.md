## What is a breakthrough?

Not every exciting result is a breakthrough. Use this ladder ::est:: — and use it on us, too.

:::ladder The evidence hierarchy — climb before you announce
<span class="n">1</span> **Anomaly** — an unexplained event in one dataset; a single odd blip.

<span class="n">2</span> **Candidate** — survives re-analysis; an initial biosignature candidate lives here.

<span class="n">3</span> **Independent confirmation** — other teams, other instruments; GW170817's multi-messenger chain ::est:: [R03].

<span class="n hi">4</span> **Established science** — reproduced repeatedly, fits the wider web of evidence; the accelerating expansion ::est:: [R08].
:::

:::chips
:::chip Pre-registration — fix the analysis plan before seeing data ◆
:::chip Blind analyses — hide the answer from yourself ::est::
:::chip Adversarial review — assign someone to disprove it ◆
:::chip Open data — let rivals re-run everything ::est:: [R18]
:::

:::callout Why the ladder matters to you
Breakthroughs usually arrive at rung 3+ — after years at rungs 1–2. News headlines routinely report rung 1 as if it were rung 4. Your defence (and ours): ask *who else confirmed it, and how?*
:::

**Going deeper.** The GW150914 announcement followed this ladder in public: candidate → internal cross-checks → community scrutiny → acceptance. The caution *looked* slow; it was the reason the result never had to be retracted ::est::.

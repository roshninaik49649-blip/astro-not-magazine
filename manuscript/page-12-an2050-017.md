## Atmospheric fingerprints — observation AN-2050-017

**Intro.** When a planet crosses its star, a thin sliver of starlight passes through the planet's atmosphere before reaching us. Molecules imprint absorption dips at specific wavelengths. Read the dips and you've read the air ::est::.

### Case file: AN-2050-017 ◆ (fictional, simulated — used across pp 12 and 41–44)

The figure on this page is a **SIMULATED** transmission spectrum for a fictional observation logged as **AN-2050-017**. Axis units: wavelength in µm; transit depth offset is dimensionless. The grey bars are simulated 1σ uncertainties; the orange line is a fitted model. Dips near 1.4 and 2.7 µm resemble water absorption; the feature near 4.3 µm resembles carbon dioxide ::est:: — *if* this were a real spectrum of a real planet.

**Going deeper.** Why this matters: spectra are the *only* remote way to weigh an atmosphere. The method is real and already used on hot giant planets ::est::; pushing it to Earth-sized rocky worlds is where active research lives ◆ [R02].

**Advanced corner.** Watch the degeneracies: a 1.4 µm dip can mean water, methane wings, or cloud slope effects ◆. Real analyses fit families of models and report Bayesian evidence, not single answers. When someone announces "they found life" from one bump — check p 14 and p 24 first.

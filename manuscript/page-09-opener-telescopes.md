# CHAPTER 02
## Eyes beyond Earth

Almost everything we know about the Universe arrived as light — faint, stretched, sometimes billions of years old. Telescopes are time machines that never move: they point at the past ::est::.

This chapter follows the light. First the machines themselves (p 10) — how sensitivity and resolution are bought with physics, not money alone. Then the different windows: optical, radio, infrared, X-ray (p 11), each showing a different Universe.

Then the payoff. Light can be intercepted mid-journey (p 12): when a planet crosses its star, a whisper of starlight filters through its atmosphere — our best clue to whether that world is anything like home (pp 13–15).

And once a whisper becomes data, you need people who can tell a signal from a smudge. That's you, on p 16.

**Key idea:** telescopes don't change the Universe; they change the *resolution* at which we see it.

## A thousand Earths?

**Intro.** The confirmed exoplanet count has climbed past five thousand ::est:: [R01] — discovered mainly by two workhorse methods: **transits** (a star dims slightly as a planet crosses it) and **radial velocity** (the star wobbles). By 2050 ▲ the catalogue should plausibly exceed tens of thousands.

**Going deeper.** But "Earth-sized" is not "Earth-like":

- We measure radius, orbit and sometimes mass — *not* landscapes ::est::
- "Habitable zone" means a temperature range where liquid water is possible, not guaranteed ::est::
- Small planets are the hardest to detect; our sample skews easy, not representative ◆

:::pull
"Earth-sized" is a measurement. "Earth-like" is a story — and stories need evidence.
:::

:::callout Analogy limits — read before dreaming
A planet with the right size and orbit could still be airless (like the Moon), hazy (like Titan) or infernally windy (like many hot Neptunes). Atmosphere, geology, magnetic field and history matter as much as distance from the star ◆.
:::

**Advanced corner.** Selection effects are the hidden error bar of the whole field. Transit surveys find close-in, large planets more easily — so raw statistics overstate such worlds until corrected. Mission teams publish detection efficiency curves for exactly this reason ◆ [R01].

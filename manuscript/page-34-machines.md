## Can machines discover physics?

**Intro.** Machine learning now does real astronomical work ::est::: classifying variable stars, detecting exoplanet transits, cleaning survey images, ranking millions of candidates for human review [R12].

**Pattern recognition vs validation — the line that matters ::est:::**

- **What AI does well:** finding statistical regularities in oceans of data; compressing a million light curves into a handful of interesting ones.
- **What AI cannot yet do:** *knowing* that a pattern means physics. A model trained on artefacts will confidently "discover" artefacts forever ::est::.

**Going deeper.** The failure mode has a name: shortcut learning ◆. Examples from the field's experience: models keying on telescope-specific pixel patterns rather than astrophysics; pipelines "detecting" the same processing glitch in a thousand frames ◆. Validation requires the things machines don't have — controlled data, physical priors, and scepticism.

**Advanced corner.** Promising research direction ◆: *interpretable* models that expose which features drive a classification, plus anomaly detectors tuned to flag "weird" rather than classify "known". The goal isn't machine physics — it's machine shortlisting with auditable reasons ▲ [R12].

**2050 forecast ▲:** discovery pipelines will be AI-first at the shortlisting stage and human-first at the validation stage ::est:: — the hierarchy on p 24 doesn't automate away its rungs.

**Caption.** A machine can find the needle; convincing anyone it's a needle is still a human act ::est::.

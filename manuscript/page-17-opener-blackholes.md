# CHAPTER 03
## Beyond the event horizon

Here the Universe keeps its secrets close. A black hole is matter curved so deeply that light itself cannot climb out ::est::. You cannot see one. You can only see what it does to everything around it.

This chapter is about *doing*: accretion disks blazing brighter than galaxies (p 18), stars orbiting invisible partners, and — since 2015 — spacetime itself ringing like a struck bell when black holes collide ::est:: [R03].

Then the chapter widens. Black holes are showpieces; the real mass of the invisible Universe is subtler. Most matter in galaxies doesn't shine at all (p 21) ::est::. Space expands faster every aeon, pushed by something we call dark energy (p 22) ::est::. And our two best ways of measuring the cosmos disagree by a few percent — the Hubble tension (p 23) ◆.

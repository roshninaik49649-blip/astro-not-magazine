## Studying black holes

**Intro.** No telescope photographs a black hole's interior — nothing escapes. But the surroundings speak volumes ::est::.

**Three ways we "see" them:**

1. **Accretion.** Gas spiralling inward heats to millions of degrees and glows in X-rays ::est::; flickering timing patterns betray the orbiting flow.
2. **Stellar choreography.** In the Milky Way's centre, stars are tracked whipping around an invisible four-million-solar-mass object at thousands of km/s ::est:: — the Nobel-winning proof that a supermassive black hole anchors our galaxy.
3. **Direct imaging.** In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope combined radio dishes across the planet into one Earth-sized virtual eye and captured the glowing ring of M87's black hole — light bent around the shadow ::est:: [R04].

**Going deeper.** The image's bright ring is emission from the accretion flow, gravitationally lensed; the dark centre is the shadow. Simulations of plasma general-relativity magneto-hydrodynamics were fitted to the data — the image and the theory were built together ::est:: [R04].

**Advanced corner.** The shadow's diameter tests general relativity at strong-field limits. So far it agrees ::est::; deviations would be revolutionary (p 24's evidence hierarchy applies). By 2050 ▲ expect sharper images and movies of orbiting gas — refinements, not new physics.
